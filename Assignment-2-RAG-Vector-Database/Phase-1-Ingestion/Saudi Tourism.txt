Saudi Tourism 
1. AlUla: Ancient Heritage and Desert Landscapes

AlUla is one of Saudi Arabia’s most distinctive tourism destinations, combining ancient heritage, dramatic desert landscapes, and modern cultural experiences. Located in northwestern Saudi Arabia, AlUla has been an important meeting point for civilizations and trade routes for thousands of years. One of its most famous attractions is Hegra, the first Saudi site to be inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. Visitors can also explore AlUla Old Town, which contains hundreds of traditional mud-brick houses, and Elephant Rock, a natural sandstone formation shaped by erosion. AlUla also offers hiking, stargazing, hot-air balloon experiences, cultural events, and modern attractions such as Maraya. The destination is particularly attractive to visitors interested in archaeology, history, nature, photography, and cultural tourism.

2. Diriyah: The Birthplace of the First Saudi State

Diriyah is a historic destination located on the outskirts of Riyadh and is strongly connected to the history and identity of Saudi Arabia. It was the capital of the First Saudi State and is home to At-Turaif District, a UNESCO World Heritage Site known for its traditional Najdi mud-brick architecture. Visitors can explore historic buildings and narrow pathways while learning about the region’s political and cultural history. Diriyah has also developed into a modern tourism destination with restaurants, cultural spaces, shopping areas, and entertainment experiences. Bujairi Terrace overlooks Wadi Hanifah and provides visitors with dining and leisure experiences inspired by Saudi culture. The combination of historical architecture, traditional design, contemporary hospitality, and cultural activities makes Diriyah an important destination for both heritage tourism and modern tourism in Riyadh.

3. Jeddah Historic District: Al-Balad

Al-Balad, the historic district of Jeddah, represents an important part of the cultural and architectural heritage of western Saudi Arabia. The district developed as a major urban center connected to the Red Sea and the pilgrimage routes leading to Makkah. Its traditional buildings are characterized by old stone structures, wooden windows, and decorative wooden balconies known as rawasheen. Walking through Al-Balad allows visitors to experience historic streets, traditional markets, old houses, mosques, and cultural landmarks. The area also contains traditional shops and local food experiences that reflect the history of Jeddah as a commercial and multicultural port city. Today, Al-Balad attracts visitors interested in history, architecture, photography, shopping, and cultural experiences, while restoration projects aim to preserve its historical character and make the district accessible to new generations.

4. Abha and Rijal Almaa

Abha is a mountainous destination in the Asir region of southwestern Saudi Arabia, known for its cooler climate, green landscapes, and distinctive local culture. The surrounding mountains provide opportunities for hiking, sightseeing, and enjoying panoramic views. One of the region’s most notable heritage destinations is Rijal Almaa, a historic village located approximately 45 kilometers from Abha. Rijal Almaa is recognized for its tall stone buildings, traditional architecture, and cultural heritage. Some structures feature basalt stone walls and white quartz decorations, while the village museum contains traditional objects, manuscripts, agricultural tools, and other artifacts. The village also reflects the social structure and lifestyle of the local community through its traditional architecture. Visitors can explore the historic village, learn about Asiri culture, enjoy mountain scenery, and experience traditional products such as local honey.

5. Farasan Islands: Marine Life and Coastal Tourism

The Farasan Islands are an archipelago in the Red Sea off the southwestern coast of Saudi Arabia. The islands are known for their beaches, clear waters, coral reefs, and diverse marine environment. Farasan offers a different type of tourism experience compared with Saudi Arabia’s desert and mountain destinations, focusing more on coastal activities and marine exploration. Visitors can enjoy boat trips, snorkeling, diving, fishing, and wildlife observation. The surrounding waters support a variety of marine species, making the islands interesting for nature lovers and photographers. The islands also have cultural and historical features, including traditional buildings and evidence of their long relationship with Red Sea trade and fishing communities. Farasan is therefore significant for both environmental tourism and cultural tourism, offering visitors an opportunity to explore Saudi Arabia’s coastal landscapes and marine ecosystems.

6. Al Ahsa Oasis: A Green Landscape in Eastern Saudi Arabia

Al Ahsa Oasis is one of Saudi Arabia’s most distinctive natural and cultural destinations. Located in the Eastern Province, the oasis is famous for its extensive date palm farms, natural springs, traditional agricultural areas, and historic settlements. Water from natural springs has supported agriculture in the region for centuries, allowing communities to cultivate crops in an otherwise arid environment. Date palms are particularly important to the identity and economy of Al Ahsa. Visitors can explore the oasis, traditional markets, historic neighborhoods, and cultural landmarks in Al Hofuf and surrounding areas. The region also provides an example of how communities adapted to the desert environment through water management and agriculture. Al Ahsa is especially interesting for visitors interested in nature, food, agriculture, history, and traditional Saudi lifestyles.

7. The Red Sea: Coastal Tourism and Island Experiences

Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea coastline offers a combination of beaches, coral reefs, islands, and marine ecosystems that are increasingly becoming part of the country’s tourism sector. The western coastline provides opportunities for diving, snorkeling, sailing, fishing, and marine exploration. The Red Sea is also known for its coral reefs and diverse marine life, making it attractive to visitors interested in underwater activities. Several tourism developments along the coast aim to create high-quality resorts and nature-based experiences while emphasizing environmental protection. The region offers a different tourism environment from inland destinations such as Riyadh, AlUla, and Abha. Visitors can experience coastal landscapes, clear waters, marine wildlife, and island environments while exploring one of Saudi Arabia’s major natural resources. Red Sea tourism is also an important part of Saudi Arabia’s plans to diversify its tourism economy.

8. Edge of the World: A Natural Landmark Near Riyadh

Edge of the World, also known as Jebel Fihrayn, is a dramatic natural landmark located in the Tuwaiq Mountain range northwest of Riyadh. The site is famous for its high cliffs and wide views over the surrounding desert landscape. The geological formations were shaped over long periods by erosion and geological processes, creating steep rock walls and exposed layers of sediment. Visitors commonly travel to the area for hiking, photography, camping, and outdoor exploration. The destination is particularly popular around sunrise and sunset because of the changing colors of the sky and landscape. Because the area is remote and has limited services, visitors need to prepare carefully and use suitable vehicles and equipment. Edge of the World is an example of how Saudi Arabia’s natural landscapes can support adventure tourism close to the capital.

9. Tabuk: Mountains, Deserts, and Natural Landscapes

Tabuk is a region in northwestern Saudi Arabia known for its combination of mountains, deserts, valleys, and historical sites. Its location near the northern Red Sea gives the region a distinctive landscape compared with central Saudi Arabia. Tabuk is associated with several natural attractions, including mountain areas and desert formations that attract visitors interested in outdoor activities and photography. The region also has historical importance because of its location along ancient routes connecting different parts of the Arabian Peninsula. Visitors can explore natural landscapes, archaeological areas, and traditional communities while experiencing the region’s different climate and geography. Tabuk has also become increasingly associated with major tourism developments in northwestern Saudi Arabia. Its combination of natural scenery, historical heritage, and emerging tourism projects makes the region an important destination for travelers looking for less traditional tourism experiences.

10. NEOM: Future Tourism in Northwestern Saudi Arabia

NEOM is a major development region in northwestern Saudi Arabia that aims to create new urban, environmental, technological, and tourism destinations. The region includes diverse landscapes such as mountains, deserts, and the Red Sea coastline. NEOM has attracted international attention because of its focus on innovative infrastructure, sustainability, technology, and new approaches to urban development. Its tourism vision includes destinations designed around nature, luxury hospitality, adventure, and marine environments. The region is also connected to Saudi Arabia’s broader Vision 2030 goals, particularly economic diversification and the development of new tourism opportunities. Unlike traditional Saudi destinations that are primarily known for established historical sites, NEOM represents a future-oriented approach to tourism. It is therefore useful for understanding how Saudi Arabia is combining natural resources, technology, investment, and tourism development to create new destinations.
