Saudi heritage and culture

1. Saudi Arda: A Traditional Cultural Performance

Saudi Arda is one of the most well-known traditional performances in Saudi Arabia and is strongly associated with national celebrations and cultural events. The performance combines rhythmic movement, poetry, drums, and traditional swords. Participants usually stand in organized rows and perform coordinated movements while chanting or reciting traditional poetry. Historically, Arda was connected to celebrations and expressions of unity, courage, and strength. Over time, it became an important cultural symbol and is now performed during national occasions, festivals, weddings, and other celebrations. Saudi Arda reflects the importance of poetry, music, collective participation, and oral traditions in Saudi culture. Its continued presence in modern Saudi society demonstrates how traditional performing arts can be preserved while remaining relevant to contemporary cultural events.

2. Saudi Coffee: A Symbol of Hospitality

Saudi coffee is an important part of traditional hospitality and social life in Saudi Arabia. It is commonly prepared using lightly roasted coffee beans and may be flavored with ingredients such as cardamom, depending on regional and family traditions. Coffee is traditionally served in a dallah, a distinctive Saudi coffee pot, and poured into small cups called finjan. Offering coffee to guests is considered a welcoming gesture and is often accompanied by dates or other traditional foods. The way coffee is served can also reflect social customs, including the practice of serving guests from the right side and refilling their cups. Saudi coffee represents more than a beverage; it is connected to generosity, respect, social interaction, and welcoming visitors. Because of its cultural importance, Saudi coffee has become a recognized symbol of Saudi hospitality.

3. Sadu Weaving: Traditional Bedouin Textile Art

Sadu is a traditional form of weaving historically practiced by Bedouin communities in the Arabian Peninsula, including communities in Saudi Arabia. The craft uses geometric patterns, strong colors, and repeated designs to create textiles and decorative objects. Traditionally, Sadu weaving was used to produce items such as tent panels, carpets, cushions, bags, and camel decorations. The patterns can reflect elements of the desert environment and the visual traditions of the communities that produced them. Sadu was traditionally created using wool from sheep and goats, as well as other natural materials available to local communities. The craft requires skill in preparing fibers, spinning yarn, arranging patterns, and operating traditional looms. Today, Sadu is preserved through cultural organizations, museums, artisans, and contemporary designers who incorporate traditional patterns into modern products.

4. Traditional Saudi Clothing

Traditional Saudi clothing reflects the country’s climate, regional diversity, social customs, and cultural identity. For men, the thobe is one of the most recognizable forms of traditional clothing and is commonly worn in everyday life as well as formal settings. Men may also wear a ghutra or shemagh, usually secured with an agal. Women's traditional clothing varies considerably between regions and can include embroidered dresses, colorful fabrics, decorative accessories, and garments designed for specific occasions. Traditional clothing in different parts of Saudi Arabia can contain distinctive patterns, embroidery styles, colors, and materials. Clothing can also change according to celebrations, weddings, religious occasions, and everyday activities. Although modern fashion is widespread, traditional Saudi garments remain an important part of national celebrations and cultural events.

5. Traditional Saudi Cuisine

Saudi cuisine reflects the geography, history, agriculture, and cultural diversity of the Arabian Peninsula. Traditional dishes vary between regions because communities have historically used ingredients that were locally available. Rice, dates, wheat, meat, dairy products, vegetables, and spices are common elements in many Saudi dishes. Kabsa is one of the most widely recognized dishes and typically combines rice with meat, spices, and other ingredients. Jareesh, Saleeg, Matazeez, and various types of traditional bread are also associated with different Saudi regions. Dates have a particularly important role in Saudi food culture and are commonly served with coffee to guests. Food is closely connected to hospitality, family gatherings, weddings, religious occasions, and celebrations. Traditional cuisine therefore provides insight into both the environmental conditions and social traditions of Saudi communities.

6. Najdi Architecture: Mud-Brick Heritage of Central Arabia

Najdi architecture developed in the central region of the Arabian Peninsula and is particularly associated with historic settlements around Riyadh and the Najd region. Traditional buildings were often constructed using mud bricks, which provided insulation against the region’s hot and dry climate. Thick walls, small windows, courtyards, and decorative geometric elements were common features of traditional Najdi houses. Buildings were designed to provide privacy and protection from the desert environment while supporting family and community life. The architecture can be seen in restored historic areas such as At-Turaif in Diriyah and other traditional settlements. Najdi architecture is significant because it demonstrates how local communities adapted construction techniques and building materials to their environment. Modern preservation projects increasingly incorporate traditional architectural elements into contemporary cultural and urban developments.

7. Traditional Saudi Markets and Souqs

Traditional markets, commonly known as souqs, have historically played an important role in commercial and social life across Saudi Arabia. These markets were places where communities could purchase food, clothing, spices, perfumes, jewelry, textiles, household items, and handmade products. Markets also served as meeting points where people exchanged news, maintained social relationships, and interacted with traders from different regions. Historic markets could be found in major settlements and along important trade and pilgrimage routes. Some traditional markets have survived or been restored and continue to operate as cultural and commercial destinations. Souqs are particularly valuable for understanding the relationship between trade and community life in Saudi Arabia. They also preserve traditional crafts and products that might otherwise disappear from everyday use.

8. Traditional Handicrafts in Saudi Arabia

Traditional handicrafts represent a wide range of skills that have been passed between generations in different regions of Saudi Arabia. These crafts include pottery, basket weaving, palm-leaf products, woodwork, metalwork, embroidery, textile production, and traditional jewelry. Local materials often influenced the development of each craft. For example, palm leaves were widely used in areas where date palms were abundant, while clay was used for pottery and wool was used for textiles. Handicrafts historically served practical purposes, providing communities with household objects, clothing, containers, tools, and decorative items. Today, artisans and cultural initiatives continue to preserve these skills while adapting some traditional designs for modern markets. Traditional handicrafts provide an important connection between Saudi cultural heritage, local materials, traditional knowledge, and contemporary creative industries.

9. Saudi Majlis: A Space for Social Interaction

The majlis is an important social institution in Saudi culture and traditionally serves as a place where family members, friends, neighbors, and guests gather. A majlis can be located inside a home or in a separate area designed specifically for receiving visitors. Traditionally, guests may be offered Saudi coffee, dates, tea, and other refreshments while conversations take place. The majlis has historically been used for social visits, discussions, celebrations, and community interaction. Its design and customs can vary between regions and families, but hospitality and respect for guests remain central concepts. The majlis also provides an example of how architecture and social customs are connected in Saudi culture. Although modern homes may use contemporary designs, the concept of the majlis remains common and continues to play a significant role in Saudi social life.

10. Traditional Music and Folk Arts in Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia has a diverse range of traditional music and folk arts influenced by the country’s different regions and communities. These traditions include forms of poetry, drumming, singing, dance, and communal performances. Different areas developed their own styles based on local history and social practices. In the western regions, for example, musical traditions can reflect the historical influence of Red Sea trade and cultural exchange, while central and northern regions have their own distinctive poetic and musical traditions. Traditional performances are often presented during weddings, national celebrations, festivals, and cultural events. Poetry is particularly important in Saudi cultural heritage and has historically been used to preserve stories, values, praise, and historical memories. Traditional music and folk arts continue to provide a connection between contemporary Saudi society and older forms of cultural expression.
